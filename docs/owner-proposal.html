The short version

Most software for this trade picks one thing: an auction site, or a shipping company's booking page. Your business is not one thing. You take in cars from salvage auctions, garage cleanouts, estates, foreclosures, liquidators and trade-ins. You take in pallets, bundles and single items. You ship it all to Tema. You clear it. You get paid.

This platform runs all of that as one operation. One place where a lot is filed, bid on, closed, paid for, shipped, cleared and handed over.

Four ways this makes money — all in one system

  1. Vehicle auctions. Salvage write-offs, garage-sale finds, repos, estate cars, trade-ins.
  2. Goods auctions. Whole pallets, bundles and single items — a 480-carton pallet and a bed frame are both lots, filed the same way.
  3. Parcel freight. Customers shipping parcels and small pallets from the US and Canada to Tema. They do not need to buy a car to use you.
  4. Vehicle freight and clearance. Quote, book, ship and clear a car from the auction house to the buyer's hands.

A car buyer and a parcel sender are different customers with different needs. Both are served by the same platform, and both are on the same books.

The auction floor takes anything you can sell

This is the part most auction software gets wrong. It assumes everything is a car. Your business is not that narrow, and the system is not either.

  • Vehicles and goods are both first-class lots. A lot is either a vehicle or goods — a pallet, a bundle, or a single item. The operator's form swaps to the right fields automatically.
  • Bulk lots carry their manifest. A pallet lot records what is inside it, how many cartons, the grade, and its dimensions. The same table holds a single headlamp, with the bulk fields simply left empty.
  • Provenance is recorded on every lot. Garage sale. Foreclosure. Liquidation. Overstock and returns. Estate. Salvage auction. Trade-in.
  • Buyers filter by it. The storefront boards are real: Vehicles, Garage goods, Salvage write-offs, Garage-sale lots. Buyers narrow by kind, provenance, city, condition and make.

The point is not that the platform has categories. The point is that when a new kind of stock arrives — an estate clearance next month, a container of returns the month after — it is filed the same way, on the same system, with the same auction and the same books. You do not buy another piece of software each time the business changes shape.

Bidding that can be proven

Every bid is a record: who, how much, and the exact second it arrived.

  • Bids are written to the database, not painted on a screen. The lot's current price and bid count are rebuilt from the bid rows themselves, so the two cannot drift apart.
  • A leading bid is demoted, never deleted. When someone outbids the leader, the old bid stays in the history marked as outbid. The full story is always there.
  • The clock defends itself. A bid in the closing window extends the auction, so no one steals a car by waiting until everyone is asleep.
  • A bidder cannot bid against themselves. The system refuses it.

When a customer says "I bid more than that," you open the record. The conversation is over in ten seconds. That is what an auction is worth.

Closing that does not depend on someone remembering

The closing logic is built and tested: at the end of an auction it checks the reserve, sells to the recorded winner if the reserve is met, and marks the lot "reserve not met" if it is not.

Two safeguards matter here.

  • A car is never sold to nobody. If the figures say a lot met its reserve but no real bidder is recorded against it, the system refuses to invent a sale and flags the lot for a human instead. One real defect was found and fixed this way.
  • Closing twice changes nothing. Running the close again on a closed lot is safe.

The scheduling that runs the closer every minute is the next switch to turn on — the hard part, the decision logic, is done and tested.

Freight: cars, parcels and pallets on the same corridor

Cars

A buyer enters the vehicle. The system knows its weight, applies the current rate, and returns a real shipping quote — Tema corridor, landed cost. No phone call, no waiting for the office to open.

Parcels and small pallets

This is a separate business line, and it is fully built.

  • Real quotes from real inputs. Weight, length, width, height, and whether the customer wants pickup. Volumetric weight is calculated, not guessed.
  • Origins that are actually served. Newark, Houston, Savannah, Los Angeles, Toronto, Vancouver — each zone is either priced or honestly says "we do not ship from here yet."
  • A booking can be re-priced from the record alone. The system stores the original enquiry, not just a total from someone's browser. A quote can be rebuilt months later and it will match.
  • The booking has a real lifecycle. Quoted, payment pending, booked — and separately expired (a link nobody took) and abandoned (nobody ever answered). Those are two different failures with two different fixes, and the system tells them apart.
  • Unpaid bookings clean themselves up. A scheduled sweep expires stale quotes hourly, so the pipeline does not fill with ghosts.

A parcel customer never has to look at a car. That matters: it is revenue that does not depend on the auction floor.

Clearance

Duty and clearance cost is estimated before the goods leave, not discovered at the port. The buyer knows what they owe before they commit.

The money is on the books, not in someone's head

  • Every buyer has an account. Money in and money out is recorded as double-entry ledger lines.
  • The system proves its own arithmetic. At any moment it can reconcile every wallet against its own ledger, to the cent.
  • The operations screen shows where cash is at risk. Cars won but unpaid. Cars paid for but not booked onto a shipment. Those are the two lists where money is quietly sitting in someone else's hands, and for the first time both are on one screen.

The hold, release and capture operations are built and tested. Wiring them to the moment of a bid is the next step — the accounts they will move already exist and already balance.

Three screens, each for the person who needs it

  • Operations console. Everything needing action today: lots, bids, shipments, clearance, exceptions, money at risk.
  • Freight console. A separate workspace for the shipping team — bookings, milestones, allocations, exceptions.
  • Customer portal. The buyer signs in and sees their cars, their parcels, their payments and their documents. The "where is my car" phone calls stop.

Behind them, three roles. A buyer sees only their own business. An operator runs the day but cannot move money out of a disputed state. The owner sees everything and holds the money authority. The difference between an operator and the owner is money and trust — nothing else.

Depth you can measure

This is not a set of screens mocked up for a meeting.

  • 525 automated checks run over the system every time it changes. They catch the things that cost money: a bid that goes missing, a wallet that stops balancing, a lot sold twice, a booking that cannot be re-priced.
  • Every surface was checked in a real browser, screen by screen — the storefront, the operations console, the freight console, the customer portal.
  • The data is real. Over 160 lots in the catalog today, with bid histories written as records rather than painted numbers.

The usual failure in software is not a missing feature. It is a system that looks finished and quietly does the wrong thing with your money. A system that checks itself is worth more than one that merely looks complete.
